## 0. The one-paragraph version

The camera clock is now **20 minutes fast**, not 15. The killer is still
**Natalie**, but the murder is no longer done in front of him — it is **set up
before anyone ate**. Corrected, every guest is out of the flat before the
eating window opens, so the question stops being "who was in the room when he
ate" and becomes "who was last alone with the food". Only Natalie was. The
answer time is **10:02 PM** and the lock code is **1002**.

The Photos app is gone from the phone. All seven camera stills and the two bin
photographs are now **printed paper at the desk**.

## 4. The flip — why this works

Both readings use the same window, 10:10–10:20.

**Raw stamps (wrong):** Darren out 10:04. Jasmine in the kitchen with Kai
10:08, out **10:16 — inside the window**. Ethan out 10:10, exactly as it opens.
Natalie in the kitchen 10:22, out 10:24 — **after he has already eaten.**

Raw conclusion: Jasmine was in the flat while he ate, and she told everyone she
left at 9:30. Arrest Jasmine. Natalie looks like a woman tidying up after a
party, and every piece of evidence that touches her reads as innocent.

**Corrected (−20):** Darren out 9:44. Ethan out 9:50. Jasmine out 9:56. Natalie
alone in the kitchen 10:02, out 10:04.

Corrected conclusion: **the flat was empty from 10:04**, and he did not eat
before 10:10. Nobody did it in front of him. It was left for him. The last
person alone with the food was Natalie, at 10:02. And her statement says he was
already eating when she left, which cannot be true.

**The same photograph changes meaning.** That is the whole room.

## 7. Rules for anyone who edits this room later

These are the ones that are easy to break and expensive to break.

1. **The window never touches a camera.** Derive it from the call log and the
   fridge card only. (See the boxed rule in §2.)
2. **The offset is FAST, and uniform.** One hub, one number, every camera.
   Never write "behind". Never give one camera a different offset.
3. **No raw stamp may fall after 10:30.** If it did, a player would ask why
   that person walked past a man collapsing, and the trap would point at the
   wrong person for the wrong reason.
4. **The offset must not reorder anyone.** A uniform shift preserves order, so
   the reveal can never be "the order changes". It is always "the window moves
   relative to the people".
5. **Natalie's times must be true.** Her statement's clock matches the raw
   stamps exactly. Her lie is about *what Kai was doing*, not when she left —
   otherwise the raw board exposes her in minute two.
6. **No document names the killer.** The walnut receipt is cash, no name. The
   tie to Natalie runs through her own 6:47 message, which reads as nothing
   until the receipt is found.
7. **Keep a five-minute margin.** Natalie leaves 10:04; the earliest he can
   have eaten is 10:10. Close that gap and a player will argue she was still
   there when he ate, which puts the murder back in the room.
8. **Every clock in the flat is accounted for.** The oven clock in Still 5 is
   the only in-world clock and it is a photograph. **No working clock as set
   dressing** — games run in the afternoon and a real clock shows the afternoon.
9. **The bin photos carry no timestamp.** The finder took them on his own phone
   at 10:41. Putting a stamp on them creates an eighth clock to reconcile.
10. **Anything the GM has to explain is broken.** If a team can only get there
    because the actor told them, fix the evidence instead of the script.
