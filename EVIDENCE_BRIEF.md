> For the agent generating the props, the phone text and the image prompts.
> **Full spoilers.** Read `CHANGES_FOR_CLAUDE_CODE.md` first — it holds the
> timeline this file dresses. Every time below is already correct. Do not
> recalculate anything.

---

## 0. What you need to know before you write a word

**The room.** A 15-minute escape room in a flat set. A man, Kai Chen, is dead
on the floor of a real room. Four friends were at his gathering. All four say
they left before it happened. Players have his phone, the contents of his flat,
and a desk of paper. Police arrive in fifteen minutes. They fill in an arrest
warrant.

**The answer.** Natalie, his sister, at 10:02 PM. She swapped his nut-free cake
for an identical walnut one and took his EpiPen. The new will he was signing at
9 AM the next morning cut her out.

**The mechanic.** The home cameras' clock is **20 minutes fast**. Every camera
stamp reads twenty minutes later than the truth. Chat times and call-log times
are network-true and correct. Fix the cameras and the meaning of one photograph
inverts: Natalie stops looking like someone tidying up after he ate and starts
looking like the last person alone with his food.

**The trap.** Read raw, the stamps put Jasmine in the flat during the eating
window, and she lied about when she left. Every team goes for Jasmine.

**The three rules for everything you produce:**

1. **Clear, not obscure.** Difficulty comes from having to combine two clean
   facts, never from a fact being hard to read. No riddles, no ciphers, no
   small print, nothing that needs a torch.
2. **One job each.** Every prop carries one fact. If it carries two, split it.
3. **Nothing names the killer.** No document, photograph or message may say
   "Natalie" in a way that skips the clock. The receipt is paid cash.

---

## 1. Voice

Match the existing pack. That means how it **reads**, not how it looks.

- **Printed props:** clipped and functional. Uppercase labels, no articles in
  headings, blank lines rendered as underscores. `FRIDGE CARD — KAI CHEN`.
  `EVENT · CAMERA STAMP · REAL TIME`. Never chatty, never atmospheric.
- **Statements:** first person, past tense, short sentences, the flat cadence of
  someone who has been asked the same question twice. One defensive detail each.
  No one explains more than they were asked.
- **Messages:** all lowercase, no full stops at the end of lines, occasional
  missing apostrophes. People type the way they text, not the way they write.
  Kai is the only one who uses capitals, and only for names.
- **Official paper** (the solicitor's letter, the will): formal, dated,
  referenced, dull. Dullness is what makes it convincing.
- **The finder's handwriting:** the four statements and the bin-photo labels are
  his. Slightly hurried. He is not a police officer and does not sound like one.

Seven frames. Printed as one A4 contact sheet with the stamp under each, plus
a single large print of Still 5. Format each caption exactly as
`HALLWAY CAM — 22:04`. **Never burn a stamp into the image itself** — it is
printed under the frame.

| # | Cam | Stamp | Real | What is in frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HALLWAY | 22:04 | 9:44 | Darren at the door, jacket on, one hand on the latch |
| 2 | KITCHEN | 22:08 | 9:48 | Jasmine and Kai talking. **Two identical white cake boxes, closed, side by side on the counter** |
| 3 | HALLWAY | 22:10 | 9:50 | Ethan leaving, phone already at his ear |
| 4 | HALLWAY | 22:16 | 9:56 | Jasmine leaving, bag over her shoulder |
| 5 | KITCHEN | 22:22 | 10:02 | **Natalie alone.** Both boxes open, one plate in front of her, her back half to the camera. **The oven clock reads 10:02** and is legible |
| 6 | HALLWAY | 22:24 | 10:04 | Natalie at the door leaving. **A tied bin bag sits against the wall** |
| 7 | HALLWAY | 22:57 | 10:37 | The finder coming in, keys still in his hand |

**Still 5 is the whole room.** It must be genuinely ambiguous. She is not
caught doing anything — she is standing at a counter with two open boxes and a
plate. A player reading the raw stamp should be able to say "she's plating up
after he ate" and feel satisfied. Do not stage guilt into it. No hand over the
bin, no glance at the camera, no cake mid-air.

## 5. The paper

### 5.1 The two allergen cards

Small printed cards that come inside the bakery box.

**On the table, beside the cut cake — brown dot:**
```
KANDAHAR ST BAKERY
COFFEE CAKE — WALNUT
flour · butter · eggs · sugar · coffee · WALNUTS
CONTAINS NUTS
```

**In bin photo A, inside the discarded box — green dot:**
```
KANDAHAR ST BAKERY
COFFEE CAKE — NUT FREE
flour · butter · eggs · sugar · coffee
MADE IN A NUT FREE KITCHEN
```

Same typeface, same size, same layout. The only differences are the word and
the dot. That is the point: the two cakes are the same cake, and he had no way
of telling.

### 5.2 Darren's receipt (paper, kitchen counter)

### 5.5 The four statements

The finder's handwriting, one per page, name and relationship at the top.

- **ETHAN — Best Friend.** "Went downstairs just before ten and told everyone it
  was work. It wasn't. I rang him from the street so we could talk without the
  others hearing. We argued. I won't say what about. I never went back up."
- **DARREN — Business Partner.** "Brought a cake from the place on Kandahar
  Street, the nut-free one, green sticker, told him not to touch the other. Left
  about quarter to ten, early start. He was fine."
- **JASMINE — Ex-Girlfriend.** "Left at half nine. It's in the group chat. Went
  straight home."
- **NATALIE — Sister.** "Left about twenty past ten, after everyone. I put his
  cake out for him before I went. **He was eating it when I said goodnight.** He
  was fine."

> **Natalie's statement is the hinge of the room and must be written exactly as
> above.** Every time in it agrees with the raw camera stamps, so nothing about
> her reads as a lie on the first pass. The lie is the sentence in bold, and it
> only becomes a lie once a team knows he cannot have eaten before 10:10 and she
> was out of the building at 10:04. Do not add a second lie. One is enough and
> two would give her away early.

## 6. The two bin photographs

Taken by the finder on his own phone at 10:41, after he opened the tied bag by
the door and before he tied it again for the police. **No timestamp on either
print** — a stamp would create an eighth clock for players to reconcile.

**Photo A — the cake.** The bag open on the hallway floor. Inside, a white
bakery box on its side, lid open, a **whole uncut coffee cake** still in it. The
**green dot** on the box and the **NUT FREE** allergen card are both legible.
Some ordinary party rubbish around it — paper napkins, a wine cork — so it reads
as a bin and not a display.

**Photo B — the EpiPen.** Closer, same bag. An **EpiPen, cap still on, unused**,
and a crumpled till receipt, flattened enough to read the 18:42 time and the
word WALNUT.

> **Why the pen is capped.** An unused pen means it was taken from him. A used
> one would mean he reached it, which changes when he collapsed and how long he
> had, and would hand players a different and wrong timeline.
>
> **Why the bag is still in the flat.** She tied it to carry down and ran out of
> time — Kai's call was ending. Still 6 shows the bag against the wall as she
> leaves. That is the answer to the first question a sharp player asks.

## 7. Images to generate

All from the photograph of the real set, so the flat in every picture is the
flat the players are standing in. Shoot the four volunteers in the real rooms,
same day, same outfits as their suspect cards, then use those frames as the base
for the generated versions.

| File | What to generate |
|---|---|
| `still-1-hallway-2204` | Darren at the front door, jacket on, hand on the latch |
| `still-2-kitchen-2208` | Jasmine and Kai at the counter mid-conversation. **Two identical closed white boxes** between them, clearly two |
| `still-3-hallway-2210` | Ethan leaving, phone to his ear |
| `still-4-hallway-2216` | Jasmine leaving, bag on her shoulder |
| `still-5-kitchen-2222` | **Natalie alone.** Both boxes open, a plate in front of her, the **oven clock reading 10:02 and legible**. Shot so the clock is unmissable once someone looks for it |
| `still-6-hallway-2224` | Natalie at the door. **Tied bin bag against the wall** |
| `still-7-hallway-2257` | The finder coming in, keys in hand |
| `bin-a-cake` | §6, Photo A |
| `bin-b-epipen` | §6, Photo B |

**Look:** stills are high-angle, slightly wide, a little grain, fixed-camera
flatness, portrait. The bin photos are the opposite — handheld, phone flash,
slightly off level. They should not look like they came from the same device,
because they didn't.

**Prompt discipline.** Give the generator the set photograph, one sentence of
staging, and the camera position. Do not ask it for mood, drama or lighting.
Every still it dramatises is a still that tells players who to arrest.

## 8. Before you hand anything over — check these

1. Is every phone time network-true, and every camera stamp exactly 20 minutes
   ahead of §2 of the changes file?
2. Does anything anywhere derive the eating window from a camera?
3. Does any raw stamp fall after 10:30?
4. Does any document name Natalie in a way that skips the clock?
5. Does Natalie's statement agree with the raw stamps in every respect except
   the one sentence about him eating?
6. Is the oven clock in Still 5 legible in the printed size, not just on screen?
7. Are the two allergen cards identical except for one word and one colour?
8. Is the EpiPen capped?
9. Does any prop show a time that isn't in §2 of the changes file?
10. Could a player solve the room without ever correcting the clock? If yes,
    something is over-explained. Find it and take it out.
